# Copy log: Willi's Walkabouts

## Voice profile (from their own pages)

Russell Willis writes plainly, in the first person plural, to experienced walkers, and does not sell. He warns as often as he invites: "Don't even think about booking until you've read the detailed trip notes", "If you don't have the patience to scroll through the entire document, this trip is not for you", "A positive mental attitude is essential". Facts are exact (temperatures in both scales, pack weights, hours per day, "Section 1 is $2495") and the pleasures are physical: "swim after swim in clear, tropical pools", "camp under the most amazing star show on earth", "a three course meal by the campfire". Site copy keeps the plainness, the warnings and the exact numbers, and drops the site builder's shouting capitals and the double dashes.

## Thesis (B3)

The one thing only Willi's Walkabouts has: they begin where the four wheel drive tours stop. Forty years of off track expeditions into Kakadu, the Kimberley, the Pilbara and the Red Centre, to places no vehicle and no other tour can reach, carrying everything on your back.

Three hero options, all from intake facts:

1. **"We begin where the four wheel drive tours stop."** Support: Off track bushwalking expeditions in Kakadu, the Kimberley, the Pilbara and the Red Centre since 1986. Four to twelve walkers, one to six weeks, everything on your back. (chosen; it is their own line, from the What to Expect page)
2. **"Trek through a wilderness where no vehicle can ever go."** Support: Their home page line. Kept as the positioning heading.
3. **"Forty years off the track in the north."** Support: Russell Willis founded the Darwin Bushwalking Club in 1974 and ran his first paying trip in 1984.

Facts used: "We begin where the four wheel drive tours stop" and "trek through a wilderness where no vehicle can ever go" (what to expect and home pages), started 1986 as a one man, one vehicle operation (guides page), first paying customer February 1984 (RW40th PDF), groups four to twelve plus guides (what to expect), trips of three or four days to six weeks (same), satellite phone and EPIRB on all major expeditions (wet season page).
